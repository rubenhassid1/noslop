# No slop.

Claude: you were sent here to make an artifact, or to fix one. Follow this page. You need nothing else from this repo.

## 1. Know who reads it

Before you build, you need two answers:

1. Who will read this page, and what do they already know? Assume they know nothing.
2. What should they do or decide when they finish?

If the request doesn't answer both, ask them as clickable questions. Ask nothing else. Then build.

## 2. The rules

1. **Say what it is, first.** The top of the page says, in one or two lines, what this is, why they got it and what to do with it. A stranger gets it in 5 seconds.
2. **Write half as much.** Then cut again. One idea per line. If a sentence can go and nothing is lost, it goes.
3. **Plain words.** Short sentences. Words a 12-year-old knows. No jargon. If a term must stay, explain it once, in a few words.
4. **No extra labels.** No taglines, no subtitle under each heading, no helper text, no secondary labels, no "tip" boxes. If a screen needs them to make sense, fix the layout.
5. **One way to read it.** One column, top to bottom. Number what the reader must do in order. No grid of tiles, cards or boxes, unless they compare things of the same kind.
6. **One screen at a time.** No long scroll. If it doesn't fit on about one screen, split it into steps or tabs the reader clicks through. Most pages need fewer parts, not more.
7. **Design it like a Google Doc.** One font. Three sizes: title, heading, text. Dark text on white. One accent color, only for what they click or must notice. Lots of space. No gradients, shadows, emoji, icons, badges or pills.
8. **Show, don't tell.** A key number goes big, not inside a sentence. A comparison goes in a table. A trend goes in one simple chart. Only when it's faster to read than words.
9. **Everything earns its place.** Take each piece out in your head. If the page still works without it, delete it.

## 3. Fixing an artifact

You got a page: a link, a file, code or a screenshot. Keep every fact, number and name. Rebuild it with the rules above. Add nothing new. When you're done, say in one line what you cut.

## 4. Check before you show it

Read it as the person from step 1, with zero context:

- In 5 seconds, can they say what it is and what to do?
- Is there one obvious place to start?
- One font, three sizes, one accent color?

If any answer is no, fix it first.
